how the plant runs
the plant runs on the same scan as the logic: transmitters write their tank's level into their tag before each scan, the logic runs, then valves and pumps move flow and tanks integrate it

valve opens to its tag's value (0–100) — with a position tag it draws as an automated valve, without one it is a hand valve at a constant opening · pump runs from a BOOL tag · LT publishes tank level · FT publishes the flow through a valve or pump · LSH/LSL publishes TRUE when its tank crosses the trip point — wire it into an interlock · flows animate where liquid moves · a full tank with net inflow shows overflow

controllers declare a make and model from the device catalog — identity today, and the anchor for vendor dialect translation on the roadmap

a controller is a device too: select it and press open logic (or double-click it) to edit the program it runs · the Logic view shows which program you are editing — a controller's, or the free-standing sandbox

try the level control plant, watch it settle near SP — then load the sign error logic example and watch the same plant run away · the workspace is saved in this browser and travels as a .llp project file; a .st file carries logic alone

about the Purdue model
the Purdue model (ISA-95) is how OT networks are drawn: the process at the bottom, business systems at the top, and progressively less trust as you go up

Level 0 field instruments and final elements · Level 1 controllers · Level 2 HMI and engineering workstations · Level 3 operations, historians · DMZ the broker that lets IT and OT exchange data without touching · Level 4 the business network

this is the same project you drew on the P&ID — a tank in Level 0 here is the tank on the P&ID, and a controller you place in Level 1 is the one running your logic

a firewall is a device on a conduit, the way a valve sits in a pipe · conduits carry a protocol (Modbus TCP, EtherNet/IP, DNP3, PROFINET, OPC UA) — simulated traffic on them is the next step on the roadmap
